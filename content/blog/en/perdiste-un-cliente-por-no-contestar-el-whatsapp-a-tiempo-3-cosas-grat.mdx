## You Lost a Customer Because You Didn't Answer WhatsApp in Time: 3 Free Things You Can Do Today

It's 11:47 PM on Sunday. You open WhatsApp Business and see four unanswered messages from Saturday afternoon. One asks about availability for Tuesday. Another wants to confirm a price. The third just says "hello?". The fourth doesn't matter anymore: around 85% of those who don't receive a response never call back [1], and 78% of customers buy from the first business that responds to them [2][3].

Those four messages probably became someone else's customers. In this article we walk through configuring the three free WhatsApp Business tools that would have prevented that: away message, greeting message, and quick replies. They don't require integrations, don't cost a cent, and take less than 15 minutes to set up.

## What Changed in 2026

Before getting into how, the context of why this matters more now:

- 62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered during business hours [4]. WhatsApp is no different: if you're cutting hair, serving a plate, or working the counter, your phone is in your pocket on silent.

- 60% of customers define "immediate response" as 10 minutes or less [3]. Not 2 hours. Not "when I finish with this customer". Ten minutes. By Wednesday afternoon, that Saturday message is 72 hours old.

- WhatsApp has more than 2.7 billion users and 98% open rates [5]. Your customers check WhatsApp. The question isn't whether they saw you didn't respond; it's how many minutes they waited before writing to your competitor.

- WhatsApp Business includes two types of native automated messages: greeting message (sent on first contact or after 14 days without interaction) and away message (activated outside business hours) [6]. Both are free and configurable from the app.

## What This Looks Like in Practice: The Three Free Tools You Have Right Now

WhatsApp Business has three automatic response features that don't require API, chatbot, or a cent. Each has a different purpose. Configuring them well means the difference between "this business is closed" and "this business isn't going to answer me, I'm going with another one".

### 1. Away Message: The First Line of Defense

Unlike quick replies, which are manual shortcuts, the away message activates automatically when you're not available, without human intervention [7].

**What it does:** It's sent automatically when a customer writes to you and you can't respond immediately. It assures them that their message was received and that you'll respond [8].

**How to configure it:**

Open WhatsApp Business app. Tap More options > Business tools > Away message. Activate "Send away message" [8]. Write the message.

This is where most make the first mistake: writing something generic like "Thanks for your message, we'll respond soon". That says nothing. The customer doesn't know if "soon" is 20 minutes or 3 days.

**Better version:**

"Hi, thanks for writing to [Business Name]. We're closed until [day] at [time]. We'll respond within the first 2 hours of opening. If it's urgent, call [number]."

Three elements: when you're closed, when you'll respond, what to do if they can't wait.

**Schedule configuration:**

The smartest option for daily use is "Outside business hours": WhatsApp compares incoming messages [9] with the schedule you configured in your business profile. If the message arrives when you're "closed", the away message goes out. If it arrives when you're "open", it doesn't.

This means you need to have business hours correctly configured in Profile > Business hours. If you open Monday to Friday 9AM-6PM and Saturdays 10AM-2PM, put it exactly like that. In the standard app, your phone must be online for the automatic response to be sent [9].

### 2. Greeting Message: The First Impression You Didn't Give in Person

The greeting message is sent automatically when a customer writes to you for the first time, or when they write after a 14-day period of inactivity [10].

**What it does:** Introduces your business, sets response expectations, and can direct the customer toward information they probably need (catalog, location, common prices).

**How to configure it:**

Business tools > Greeting message. Activate it. Choose who receives it: "Everyone", "Everyone except those in your address book", "Everyone except..." or "Send only to..." [10]

**Functional example for a mechanic shop:**

"Hi, thanks for contacting [Shop Name]. We normally respond in less than 1 hour during business hours (Mon-Fri 8AM-5PM, Sat 9AM-1PM). To schedule an appointment, send us: car make, year, and what service you need. For roadside emergencies, call [number]."

Note the elements: realistic response time, schedule, what information you need to help them, alternative for emergencies.

**When NOT to activate it for everyone:**

If your business has recurring customers who write to you several times a week and haven't interacted in 14 days, they're going to receive the greeting message again. If you have a greeting message configured for "all contacts", they'll receive it if they're a new contact or haven't written in 14 days. You can avoid this by configuring the greeting message to activate only outside business hours or for contacts not in your address book [10].

### 3. Quick Replies: Not Automatic, But They Speed Up Response 10x

Quick replies are sent by a human agent selecting a shortcut. They require manual intervention but enormously speed up response time. Automatic messages activate without human intervention (greeting and away messages) [11].

**What it does:** Saves pre-written responses that you can insert by typing `/` and the shortcut you configured. They don't send themselves; you or your employee select them.

**How to configure it:**

Business tools > Quick replies > `+` button (add). To configure this, go to Business tools > Quick replies, and create a shortcut for a common response [12].

Write the message, assign a shortcut (for example `/price` or `/location`), save.

**To use it:** In any chat, type `/` and the list of quick replies appears. Select the one you need, it inserts into the text field, you can edit it if needed, and send.

**Useful examples for a barbershop:**

- Shortcut: `/schedule`  
  Message: "We're open Mon-Sat 10AM-7PM, closed Sunday. You can schedule here or call [number]."

- Shortcut: `/prices`  
  Message: "Regular cut: $25 / Cut + beard: $40 / Kid's cut: $18. We accept cash and card."

- Shortcut: `/today`  
  Message: "Today we have availability at 2PM, 4PM and 5:30PM. Which works for you?"

Quick replies can include images, videos, and documents [10]. This means you can save a quick reply with your catalog in PDF, or a photo of the menu, or the location map.

**The most common mistake:** Saving 15 quick replies with corporate writing that nobody uses because it's faster to write the response. The agent can read the conversation context before sending the response and, if necessary, edit it on the fly. It's the best of both worlds: automation speed with human control [11].

Start with 3 or 4: the questions you literally answer every day.

## When NOT to Use These Automatic Messages

Configuring automatic messages isn't the solution if:

1. **You already respond in less than 30 minutes consistently during your business hours.** If you have someone dedicated to answering the phone or messages and they already do it quickly, a greeting message can feel robotic. In that case, only configure the away message for when you close.

2. **You operate 24/7 with staff in shifts.** The away message doesn't apply if there's always someone available. Quick replies do, but the away message becomes redundant.

3. **Your business requires technical evaluation before any response.** If each inquiry needs you to review a history, inventory, or file before answering anything useful, an automatic message that says "we'll respond soon" without context can frustrate more than help. In that case, configure the away message with information about when they can expect an evaluation: "Thanks for writing. We'll review your case and respond within 24 hours with a detailed quote."

## The Next Step: When You Do Need Real Automation

The three elements we covered here are free, native to WhatsApp Business, and don't require programming. But they have hard limits:

- **Text only.** The greeting message only supports text [10]. You can't send buttons, interactive menus, or capture structured information.

- **One phone, one account.** The app only allows up to 4 linked devices. If you have more than four people handling WhatsApp, this version falls short [13].

- **No context or memory.** The away message can't say "Your appointment is Tuesday at 3PM" because it's not connected to your calendar. Quick replies don't adjust to the customer's name or the service they booked last time.

- **Zero logic.** You can't make the message change depending on whether the customer wrote "price", "appointment" or "where are you". Automatic messages are pre-written responses activated by simple rules (new contact, outside hours). A chatbot understands intent and can hold multi-step conversations [14].

If you meet two of these three conditions, you probably need the next level (API + automation):

1. You receive more than 50 customer messages per week and don't have someone dedicated to answering them.
2. You lose customers because you don't respond outside hours (nights, weekends, holidays) and you know your competition is available.
3. Your employees spend more than 1 hour daily answering the same 5 questions (price, schedule, availability, location, policies).

The WhatsApp Business app is free, but the WhatsApp Business API is designed for growing teams that need automation, integrations, and large-scale messaging. Access to the API itself is free, but you pay for WhatsApp messages you send, according to message categories, volume, and your region [15].

## What We've Seen in SMBs That Configure This Well

At Impulsa Lab we run automation implementations for SMBs, but before getting there, we always ask: did you already configure WhatsApp Business's native automatic messages?

Most haven't. And when they do, they make two mistakes:

**Mistake 1: Away message without specific schedule.**  
"Thanks for your message, we'll respond soon."  
The customer doesn't know if "soon" is 15 minutes or 2 days. Result: they assume you're not going to answer and write to another business.

**Mistake 2: Quick replies written to look professional, not to be used.**  
"Dear customer, we appreciate your interest in our services. To provide you with personalized attention..."  
Nobody on your team is going to use that. They're going to write "Hi, what do you need?" because it's faster.

The version that works is the one you actually use. If your normal tone is "Hi, thanks for writing", that's the tone of the automatic message. If you normally say "Cool, I'll write you down for Tuesday at 3", the quick reply is "Does Tuesday at 3PM work for you?".

Configure this today. Takes 15 minutes. The next time a customer writes Saturday at 8PM, they're going to know exactly when you're going to respond. And that, in 2026, is the difference between "this business is organized" and "this business isn't paying attention to me".
