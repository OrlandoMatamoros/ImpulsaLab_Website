Check your phone right now. How many WhatsApp messages went unanswered since yesterday. Five, ten, twenty. Some ask for prices, others want appointments, one wants to confirm the time. 78% of customers buy from the first business that responds. Not the cheapest, not the highest rated: the first. This article covers three free tools included in WhatsApp Business that you can set up in less than 15 minutes. You don't need a paid chatbot, you don't need API, you don't need to hire anyone. You just need to open the app and follow five steps.

## The 3 Free WhatsApp Business Tools

WhatsApp Business (the free app, not the API) includes three native automation features. All completely free within the app. No message limits, no per-user charges, no hidden upgrades.

### 1. Away Message

It's the automatic response sent when a customer contacts you outside your business hours or when you can't handle conversations. It activates automatically for anyone who writes during the hours you marked as "away" in the business hours settings.

**How to set it up:**

Go to Settings > Business tools > Away message, turn it on, write your text and configure the schedule. The whole process takes less than three minutes.

**What to write:**

An effective message has three elements: confirmation, deadline, alternative.

Bad:
"Thanks for your message. We'll respond soon."

Good:
"Thanks for writing. Our hours are Monday to Friday 9 AM to 6 PM EST. We'll respond tomorrow first thing. Urgent? Call (555) 123-4567."

The difference: the customer knows WHEN to expect a response and has an out if they can't wait. Don't say "we'll respond soon." Say "we respond Monday to Friday 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM". The customer needs to know WHEN.

If you have an FAQ page, updated hours on Google or digital catalog, include it in the message. If you're closed Sundays but the customer writes Sunday at 3 PM asking if you have availability that afternoon, your away message should say "we open tomorrow Monday at 9 AM, you can schedule here: [link]". Don't leave the customer without options.

### 2. Greeting Message

Sent automatically when a customer writes for the first time, or when they write after 14 days of conversation inactivity. It's your chance to introduce the business before the customer asks.

**How to set it up:**

Open WhatsApp Business, go to Business tools and select Greeting message. Turn on the feature, edit the default message, choose who receives it and save.

**What to write:**

Bad:
"Hello! Welcome to [business name]. How can we help you?"

Better:
"Hi, I'm Ana from [business name]. We do cuts and color in the Bronx, hours Monday to Saturday 10 AM - 7 PM. What service are you interested in?"

The greeting message should say WHAT you do, WHERE you are, WHEN you respond. If you have a digital menu, product catalog or price list, this is the place to share it: "You can see our menu here: [link]. Any questions?"

Some businesses write generic greeting messages that sound like corporate bot. That's fine for a bank, not for a hardware store or salon. The tone should sound like it was written by the person who works the counter, because that person IS who will respond when the business is open.

### 3. Quick Replies

Not completely automatic, but almost. You create shortcuts (like /hours or /price) that expand into complete messages. Useful for whoever handles live chats.

**How they work:**

You save pre-recorded messages with a short code. Example:

- `/hours` → "We're open Monday to Friday 9 AM - 6 PM, Saturdays 10 AM - 4 PM, closed Sundays."
- `/location` → "We're located at 123 Main St, Queens NY 11375. Here's the map: [link]"
- `/price` → "Men's cut: $25. Cut + beard: $40. Kids' cut: $18. Want to schedule?"

When a customer asks "how much is a cut?", you type `/price` on your keyboard, WhatsApp expands the shortcut to the full message and you send it. You don't have to write the same response 15 times a day.

**How to set them up:**

Go to Settings > Business tools > Quick replies. Create a message, assign it a shortcut (maximum 20 characters) and save. You can create as many as you need.

Quick replies aren't pure automation, but in practice they reduce response time from two minutes to ten seconds. If you handle between 20 and 50 messages daily, the accumulated difference is hours per week.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

Suppose you have a cell phone repair shop in Jackson Heights. You're closed Sundays. A potential customer searches "iPhone repair near me" on Sunday at 2 PM, finds your business on Google, clicks the WhatsApp button and writes: "Hi, I broke my iPhone screen, how much to fix it?"

**Scenario without configuration:**

The message arrives. Nobody responds because you're closed. The customer waits 10 minutes, doesn't even see a "seen", assumes you don't check WhatsApp, searches the next shop on Google and writes there. Monday at 9 AM you open WhatsApp, see the message and respond. The customer already paid 180 USD somewhere else and doesn't even open your message.

**Scenario with away message configured:**

The message arrives. In less than a second, WhatsApp automatically sends:

"Thanks for writing. We're closed Sundays. We open tomorrow Monday at 9 AM. Most screen repairs we deliver same day. Urgent? Check our prices here: [link to your catalog]. We'll write you tomorrow first thing."

The customer knows you received the message, knows when you're going to respond, knows you do same-day repairs. They might still go with the competition today Sunday because they need the phone NOW, but if they can wait until tomorrow, they wait for you. Without the away message, they assumed you never answer.

That automatic message doesn't close the sale, but keeps the customer in the funnel. It's the difference between 0% probability of conversion and 30%.

**What changes:**

You don't need to hire someone to check WhatsApp on Sundays. You don't need a chatbot that interprets questions with AI. You just need to configure a predefined message that says "I'm closed now, I'll respond tomorrow at this time". The setup takes three minutes and works 24/7 for years without intervention.

If you receive 10 messages per week outside hours, and without automatic response you lose 80% of those customers, you're letting 8 weekly opportunities go. Count how many of those customers would have an average ticket of 50, 100 or 200 USD in your business. That's the figure you're giving away by not configuring a feature you already paid for (it's free) and already have installed.

## When NOT to Use These Messages

Setting up automatic responses isn't always the right solution. There are three scenarios where the automatic message worsens the experience instead of improving it.

**1. If your business responds in less than 5 minutes all the time**

A restaurant with a hostess at the counter who checks WhatsApp every time it rings doesn't need a greeting message. The real person responds faster than the bot. The automatic message adds friction: the customer sees two responses (the automatic and the real) and gets confused about which to follow.

**2. If your away message doesn't offer a real alternative**

An away message that says "we're closed, we'll respond when we open" without giving specific hours, without a link to prices, without an emergency number, is worse than sending nothing. It tells the customer "we received your message but we're not going to help you". It's frustrating instead of useful.

Rule: if you can't include concrete response hours OR an alternative (catalog, FAQ, emergency phone), don't activate the away message. Better respond manually when you open.

**3. If your industry requires mandatory personalized consultation**

An immigration lawyer, an accountant handling complex cases, an architect: in these businesses the customer doesn't expect instant generic response, they expect someone qualified to read their case and respond with judgment. An automatic message that says "thanks for writing, we'll be in touch" sounds professional but adds no value. The customer already knows you'll respond; what they need is for someone to READ their situation.

In these cases, the greeting message can work if it sets clear expectation: "Hi, I'm [name], immigration lawyer in Queens. I review all messages personally and respond in less than 24 business hours. Please tell me your situation and I'll give you options." That's better than a generic "how can I help you?"

## The Most Common Mistake

Business owners configure the away message, activate it, test it once by sending themselves a WhatsApp from another phone, see that it works, and never review it again. Six months later the message says "we're closed for Christmas vacation" and it's already April.

The away message changes every time your schedule changes. If you close a Monday for a holiday, update the message Sunday night. If you extend hours in summer, update the message the day the change starts. If you change emergency phone number, update all three messages (away, greeting, quick replies) the same day.

Three-rule test to know if this solution works for you:

1. You receive at least 10 messages from potential customers per week on WhatsApp.
2. At least 3 of those messages arrive outside your business hours or when you're busy serving someone else.
3. You can define in one clear sentence what your business does, where it is and when you respond.

If you meet all three, configure the tools today. If you don't receive enough messages yet, the problem isn't automation, it's distribution: you need more people to know you exist before worrying about responding fast.

Open WhatsApp Business now, go to Settings > Business tools, configure the away message with real hours and concrete alternative, save, and let it run.
