78% of customers buy from the first business that responds to their inquiry ([thisandthat.chat](https://www.thisandthat.chat/blog/customer-response-time-statistics/)). Not the one with the best price, not the closest one. The first one that answers. And while you were checking your phone, they were already talking to your competition.

## What Changed

60% of customers define "immediate" as less than 10 minutes, and in 2026 that same customer who sends a WhatsApp on Saturday at 10 PM expects confirmation in minutes ([hyperleap.ai](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/smb-customer-messaging-expectations-2026)). That wasn't happening three years ago.

The WhatsApp Business app is completely free and includes away and greeting messages at no cost ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-automatic-replies)). There's no monthly limit on these automatic replies, no credit card required, no hidden premium version. If you use the Business app instead of personal WhatsApp, you already have access.

The pricing changes Meta introduced in 2026 only apply to the WhatsApp Business API; if your team responds manually in the WhatsApp Business app or WhatsApp Web, those messages remain free ([eazybe.com](https://eazybe.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing)).

Since July 2025, responses within the 24-hour customer service window count as utility messages and have been free through the API; costs apply to marketing templates outside that window ([chatarmin.com](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/whatsapp-automatic-reply)).

## How This Looks in Practice

### 1. Away Message: What Happens Outside Hours

The WhatsApp Business away message is an automatic response sent to customers when they contact you outside your business hours or when you can't handle conversations ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-away-message)).

You open WhatsApp Business. Go to **Settings > Business tools > Away message**. Turn it on. Write the text. Set the schedule: it can be "outside business hours" (uses the hours you configured in your business profile) or a custom schedule.

Setting up the away message in WhatsApp Business is a quick process that requires no technical knowledge and the whole process will take no more than three minutes ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-away-message)).

Don't say "we'll respond soon"; say "we respond Monday to Friday from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM"; the customer needs to know WHEN ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-away-message)).

Example for a barbershop:

> "Thanks for your message. We're closed until 9:00 AM Tuesday. We'll answer you then. If it's urgent, call (XXX) XXX-XXXX."

From that moment, any message that arrives outside those hours gets that response instantly. The customer knows their message arrived and when to expect a response. That prevents them from assuming you ignored them and looking for someone else.

### 2. Greeting Message: Automatic First Impression

The greeting message is sent automatically the first time a customer sends you a message or after 14 days of inactivity in the conversation ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-automatic-replies)).

Same path: **Settings > Business tools > Greeting message**. Turn it on. Write the text. Choose who receives it: everyone, only people not in your contacts, or saved contacts.

Keep greeting and away messages concise so they're easy to read at a glance; a message of 100 to 250 characters is generally enough to greet customers, communicate availability and set response expectations ([wati.io](https://www.wati.io/en/blog/enable-auto-reply-for-whatsapp-business/)).

Example for a mechanic shop:

> "Hi! Thanks for writing to [Name] Shop. We normally respond in less than 2 hours. How can we help you?"

This message doesn't solve the inquiry, but it does two things: confirms they reached the right place and establishes how long they'll wait. That lowers customer anxiety and gives you room to respond well instead of rushed.

### 3. Quick Replies: Shortcuts for Repeated Questions

WhatsApp Business quick replies are predefined messages that your agents can insert in any conversation by typing a keyboard shortcut that starts with "/" ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-quick-replies)). Unlike the previous two, **they don't send automatically**. You have to type the shortcut manually, but they save you from writing the same thing 40 times per week.

**Settings > Business tools > Quick replies > Add**. Write the shortcut (example: `/hours`), write the complete message, save. Meta includes this feature free in the WhatsApp Business app and allows storing up to 50 different quick replies ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-quick-replies)).

When someone asks "what time do you open?", you type `/hours` in the chat and the complete message appears:

> "We're open Monday to Friday from 9 AM to 7 PM, and Saturdays from 10 AM to 3 PM. What else can I help you with?"

Each quick reply can include text, attached multimedia files (images, videos, documents) and dynamic variables ([gurusup.com](https://gurusup.com/blog/whatsapp-quick-replies)).

Examples of useful shortcuts for a Latin restaurant:

- `/menu` → link to menu in PDF or photo  
- `/delivery` → "We do delivery within 3 miles, $15 minimum"  
- `/payment` → "We accept cash, card and Zelle"  
- `/reservation` → "To reserve we need name, date, time and number of people"

If you answer 20 messages per day and 12 are about the same four questions, you just cut your response time in half.

## When NOT to Use Only These Three Features

If you meet all three conditions below, stick with away message + greeting + quick replies. You don't need more:

1. **You receive less than 30 messages per day** and can answer them in batches (morning, midday, afternoon).  
2. **The questions are different each time**. There's no clear pattern that repeats in 60% of chats.  
3. **You don't sell directly through WhatsApp**. You use the channel to coordinate appointments or confirm orders you already took another way.

If you receive more volume, if most questions are variations of the same thing ("do you have X in stock?", "how much does Y cost?", "when does my order arrive?"), or if WhatsApp is your main sales channel, these three features stop the bleeding but don't close the wound. At that point you need logic: a system that reads the question, searches your inventory or calendar, and answers with real data. That no longer comes in the free app; it requires API and a workflow with something like n8n, Make or Zapier connected to your system.

## The Stack We Use at Impulsa Lab When Volume Goes Up

When a client exceeds 50-100 daily messages or needs to integrate WhatsApp with their calendar, inventory or CRM, we run this:

- **WhatsApp Business API** (not the app): any free-form response sent within a 24-hour window opened by a customer message incurs no Meta charge, regardless of volume ([montymobile.com](https://montymobile.com/newsroom/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-the-complete-2026-cost-guide-for-enterprise-teams/)). The charge appears when **you** initiate the conversation or when you respond after 24 hours. In 2026, representative rates range from approximately USD 0.0103 per message in India to USD 0.025 in the United States ([montymobile.com](https://montymobile.com/newsroom/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-the-complete-2026-cost-guide-for-enterprise-teams/)).  
- **n8n** (self-hosted, 0 USD/month on a 6 USD/month VPS from Hetzner) or **Make** (Free plan up to 1,000 operations/month, then from 9 USD/month): orchestrates the flow. Receives the message, sends it to the AI model, takes the response, sends it back to the customer.  
- **Claude Sonnet 4** or **Gemini 2.0 Flash** to understand intent and generate responses: Claude costs ~3 USD per million input tokens, ~15 USD per million output (anthropic.com/pricing, August 2026); Gemini 2.0 Flash comes free up to a certain limit and then 0.075 USD per million input tokens (ai.google.dev/pricing, August 2026).  
- **Knowledge base** in Notion, Google Sheets or Airtable (free up to certain volume): the agent queries there for prices, schedules, availability before answering.

Typical operating cost for a business doing 2,000 messages/month in the United States, half initiated by the customer (free) and half our responses after 24 hours or templates: ~50-75 USD/month in API messages + 6-20 USD in infrastructure and models. That's less than the cost of one person answering part-time, and it runs 24/7.

But that stack makes sense **only** if the three free features can't handle the volume anymore or if you need real-time decisions (check stock, schedule appointment, charge). If you can still answer in less than 2 hours during the day, start with the free stuff.

## The Most Common Mistake: Set and Forget

You activated the away message. You wrote something generic like "thanks for writing, we'll answer you soon". And you never checked if it works.

The problem: the most common causes of failure are: the smartphone is offline (the app only sends while connected), the schedule is misconfigured, or there's a recipient group selected in "Recipients" that excludes the sender ([chatarmin.com](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/whatsapp-automatic-reply)).

Check these three things every two weeks:

1. **The schedule is still correct**. If you changed opening hours and didn't update the away message, you're sending "we're closed" when you're actually open.  
2. **The message says something useful**. "Thanks for writing" doesn't help. "We respond Monday to Saturday, 9 AM to 6 PM; if you write Sunday, we'll answer you Monday before 10 AM" does help.  
3. **The phone running WhatsApp Business has stable data or wifi**. If you turn it off at night or if the signal in your location is bad, messages don't send.

## Next Executable Step

Download WhatsApp Business (Google Play or App Store). Migrate your number or use a new one. Set up away message with real schedule + greeting message with honest response time. Create three quick replies for the questions you receive most. That takes 15 minutes and cuts the problem of "I lost the customer because I didn't answer" by 60-70% before spending a dollar.
