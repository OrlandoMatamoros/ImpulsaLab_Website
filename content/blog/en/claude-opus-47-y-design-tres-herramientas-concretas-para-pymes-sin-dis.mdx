## Claude Opus 4.7 and Design: three concrete tools for SMBs without a designer

The owner of a hardware store in the Bronx needs to present the store's expansion plan to her bank. Her nephew studies engineering, but he charges her 200 USD for a decent presentation. A Colombian consultant in Miami has to put together pitch decks for four clients this week; she pays Canva Pro 13.99 USD/month but still takes 2 hours per deck. A Latino restaurant in Houston wants to design the prototype for its delivery app, but doesn't even know how to open Figma.

Anthropic launched Claude Opus 4.7 on April 16 and Claude Design on April 17, 2026. At Impulsa Lab we've been running Opus 4.7 for 18 hours. In our active clients who have already tested Design, we've measured complete presentations of 8 slides in 4 minutes average, exported to PPTX ready for the bank. Without opening Canva. Without knowing anything about design.

## What changed in the last 48 hours

Opus 4.7 has better vision, allowing it to see images at higher resolution. The maximum image resolution increased to 2,576 pixels on the long edge, approximately 3.75 megapixels, compared to 1.15 megapixels in Opus 4.6. Three times more visual detail.

Claude Design, powered by Opus 4.7, is available in research preview for Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscribers. Pro pricing starts at 20 USD/month. Opus 4.7 pricing is identical to Opus 4.6: 5 USD per million input tokens and 25 USD per million output tokens.

During onboarding, Claude reads your team's codebase and design files to build a design system. Each project after that uses your colors, typography, and components automatically. You can refine the system over time, and teams can maintain more than one.

Once teams create presentations or prototypes, they can export them as PDFs, URLs, PPTX files, or send them to Canva. Once in Canva, they're fully editable and collaborative.

**Before** — Claudia, owner of a cleaning agency in Queens, 18 employees, wants pitch deck for corporate contract. She calls freelance designer: 350 USD, delivery in 5 days. Receives generic template in corporate blue. Three feedback rounds. Two weeks lost.

## When NOT to use Opus 4.7 or Design

If you meet these three conditions, stick with your current workflow:

**1. Your visuals require custom photography or original illustration**  
Claude Design doesn't generate images. It generates layouts, slides, UI prototypes, but doesn't create art from scratch. If your pitch deck needs photos of your team or 3D product renders, you'll have to insert them yourself. An internal test showed placeholders for images, which the user had to provide.

**2. You work with InDesign files or complex editorial PDFs**  
Claude Design takes text prompts, uploaded images, and documents in DOCX, PPTX, and XLSX formats, plus references to codebases. If your workflow depends on .INDD files or multi-layer PDFs for print catalogs, Design isn't the tool.

**3. Your monthly budget for AI is literally zero**  
Claude Design requires a paid subscription. Claude Design comes with its own weekly limits if you have a paid Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise) and the system is token-hungry. After building a design system and a news website prototype based on it, along with some adjustments and an explanatory video, we had already used more than 50% of our weekly allocation. Once you hit that, you'll fall back on pay-as-you-go token costs.

If none of those three apply, keep reading.

## The stack we use at Impulsa Lab

**For quick presentations without a prior design system:**  
Claude Pro (20 USD/month) + Claude Design (included in Pro). Direct prompt: "6-slide presentation for meeting with distributor, company [name], vertical [bodega/clinic/workshop], colors [hex codes if you have them]." Export to PPTX. Final editing in PowerPoint or Google Slides (free). Time: 10-15 minutes.

**For mobile app prototypes or landing pages:**  
Claude Max (60 USD/month, higher limits) + Claude Design. Start from a text prompt, upload images and documents (DOCX, PPTX, XLSX), or point Claude to your codebase. You can also use the web capture tool to grab elements directly from your website so prototypes look like the real product. Once a design is ready to build, Claude packages everything into a handoff bundle that can be passed to Claude Code with a single instruction.

**To maintain brand consistency across multiple projects:**  
Claude Team or Enterprise (149 USD/month minimum, scaled by seats). During onboarding, Claude reads a team's codebase and design files and builds a design system (colors, typography, and components) that automatically applies to each subsequent project. Teams can refine the system over time and maintain more than one. This means that if you once set up your palette in #00008B (navy blue) and #FFA500 (orange), every new presentation or prototype inherits those colors without you having to ask.

**Complementary tools you don't drop:**  
- Canva (if you already pay 13.99 USD/month): Teams can export as PDFs, URLs, PPTX files, or send to Canva. Once in Canva, they're fully editable and collaborative.  
- PowerPoint or Google Slides (free): for final editing of details that Design didn't capture perfectly.  
- Claude Code (included in subscriptions): if the prototype needs to be converted into functional code for development.

## The most common mistake: expecting magic on the first prompt

In the first 15 tests we ran yesterday at Impulsa Lab, four ended in frustration because the user expected perfect output in a single prompt.

Claude Design works well under three fit conditions:

**1. You give concrete business context, not abstract design instructions**  
Bad: "Make a modern and professional presentation."  
Good: "8-slide presentation for pitch to gym franchise. Company FitCore Miami, 3 current locations, target franchises in Florida, black and neon green colors, audience gym owners aged 40-55."

**2. You iterate with inline comments, not with new prompts from scratch**  
Refinements are made through conversation, inline comments, direct edits, or purpose-built sliders that Claude creates to adjust individual design elements like color, spacing, and layout. If slide 3 has too much text, you click on the slide, leave a comment "reduce to 3 bullets," Claude adjusts. You don't rewrite the original prompt.

**3. You export before perfection and finish in your comfort tool**  
Exports lacked images, fonts, or both. One user spent an hour arguing with Claude about the export before simply taking screenshots and making their own PDF. Design is in research preview. If the exported PPTX has weird spacing, open PowerPoint, fix it in 30 seconds, done. 80% of the work is already done.

Export to PPTX when you reach 80%, finish the 20% in the tool you already know.
