## Claude Opus 4.7 and Design: three concrete tools for SMBs without a designer

Anthropic launched Claude Opus 4.7 on April 16, 2026. One day later, on April 17, Claude Design arrived. Between the two, we have in our hands three tools that change the game for the SMB that doesn't have a designer on staff but needs sales presentations, product prototypes, or professional-looking marketing materials.

We're not talking about replacing your designer (if you have one). We're talking about the founder of a hardware store in Queens being able to create a vendor presentation in 20 minutes. Or the manager of a dental practice in Miami exporting a prototype of her new appointment app without hiring Figma or opening Canva.

At Impulsa Lab we've been running Opus 4.7 since launch day. We've seen clients with teams of 8-15 people go from hiring freelance designers at 80 USD an hour to generating internal decks in a 10-minute conversation. The stack we describe below is operating today in five of our active clients.

## What changed in April 2026

**Opus 4.7 sees images 3× better.** Maximum resolution jumped from 1.15 megapixels to 3.75 megapixels (2,576 pixels on the long edge). That means a screenshot of your website or a high-resolution mockup no longer turns into pixelated mush.

**Claude Design exports to PPTX, PDF, Canva, and HTML.** Once presentations or prototypes are created, teams can export them as PDF, URL, PPTX files, or send them to Canva, where they're fully editable and collaborative. It's not a static PNG; it's a native file your team can open and modify.

**Zero price increase.** Pricing is identical to Opus 4.6: 5 USD per million input tokens, 25 USD per million output. The Pro plan costs 20 USD/month with access to PPTX generation from chat and the PowerPoint add-in; Team Standard starts at 25 USD/month per seat with a 5-seat minimum.

**Reads your codebase to maintain visual consistency.** Claude Design can apply a team's design system to every project it creates, so results are consistent with the company's overall visual style. Anthropic says Claude Design is capable of this by reading a company's codebase and design files. You upload your repo or brand kit in PPTX/Figma format, and Claude maintains your colors, typography, and components in everything it generates.

## When NOT to use Claude Design

This isn't Canva. It's not Figma either. If you meet these three conditions, stick with your current workflow:

**Condition 1:** Your company has an internal design team actively managing your visual identity, and every piece of external material goes through brand review. Claude Design speeds up drafts, but doesn't replace the creative judgment of a senior designer who knows the brand inside out.

**Condition 2:** You need pixel-perfect control of every element. Claude generates interactive prototypes and structured presentations, but for now, Claude Design outputs are good enough for internal presentations and quick mockups, but still require human refinement for anything production-level. If your standard is four-color offset printed brochure, this isn't your path.

**Condition 3:** Your design flow is already optimized and automated with specialized tools. If your 12-person team already has working processes with Figma + Notion + Slack and generates 50 assets per week without friction, adding Claude Design would be noise. The tool shines when you *don't* have an established design workflow.

## The most common mistake we see

The mistake isn't technical. It's about expectations.

We've seen founders open Claude Design, write "sales presentation," get 5 generic slides, and conclude the tool doesn't work. The problem wasn't Claude. It was the prompt.

The company's documentation states: "You don't need to be a designer to get excellent results. Be specific about what you're building, who it's for, and what matters most."

Three fit conditions for an effective prompt:

**Full context in text.** Don't write "marketing presentation." Write: "10-slide presentation for B2B distributor of HVAC equipment in Florida, audience: purchasing managers at hospitals, goal: demonstrate ROI of preventive vs. corrective maintenance, include 3 success stories with savings figures, comparative cost chart over 5 years."

**Reference files attached.** Upload your logo in PNG, your last presentation in PPTX (even if it's ugly), a Word document with content in plain text. You can start from a text prompt, upload images and documents (DOCX, PPTX, XLSX), or point to your codebase. You can also use the web capture tool to grab elements directly from your website.

**Conversational iteration.** The first output is never final. Ask for changes: "Slide 3, change the bar chart to trend lines. Darker colors. Title font in bold." Refinement happens through a combination of channels: chat-based conversation, inline comments on specific elements, direct text editing, and custom adjustment sliders that Claude itself generates to let users tweak spacing, color, and layout in real time.

If you follow those three conditions, the success rate of the first usable output goes from ~20% to ~75% in our active clients.

## When this does fit your SMB

Three signals that Claude Opus 4.7 + Design is the right solution today:

**Signal 1:** You generate between 5 and 30 presentations, prototypes, or one-pagers per month, and none of those pieces require approval from a corporate brand committee. You're an SMB of 5-40 people. Your materials go to clients, your internal team, or investors/partners, but don't need to pass through 4 layers of legal + compliance.

**Signal 2:** Today you delegate design to freelancers or to "the person on the team who knows PowerPoint," and the bottleneck isn't quality but speed. You need quick versions to iterate, not a final piece for offset printing.

**Signal 3:** You have content (text, data, images) but zero time or skill to assemble it visually. You're the founder, the operations manager, the sales lead. You know *what* to say, but opening Canva paralyzes you because you don't know where to start.

If you meet all three signals, try this: open Claude Design, upload your last ugly presentation and a Word document with the content you want for the next one. Write: "Using the visual style of the attached presentation, create 8 new slides with the content from the Word doc, add a quarterly evolution chart, blue/gray colors, export to PPTX." Twenty minutes later, download the file.

That's the test. If it works, cancel the next payment to the freelance designer and reassign those 4 hours/week to something else.
