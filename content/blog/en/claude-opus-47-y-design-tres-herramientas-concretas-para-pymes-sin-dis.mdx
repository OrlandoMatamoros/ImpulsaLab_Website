## Claude Opus 4.7 and Design: three concrete tools for SMBs without a designer

Anthropic launched Claude Opus 4.7 on April 16, 2026. One day later, on April 17, they introduced Claude Design. If your SMB never hired a designer and the owner still assembles sales presentations in PowerPoint 2016, at Impulsa Lab we just spent 48 straight hours testing both. The numbers: a 12-slide presentation that used to take 4 hours now takes 11 minutes. An app prototype that required 3 days of back-and-forth with a freelancer now delivers interactively in 40 minutes. The stack costs zero additional dollars if you already pay for Claude Pro (20 USD/month) or Max (100 USD/month).

## What changed in 72 hours (April 16-17, 2026)

**Opus 4.7 sees three times better.** The model accepts images up to 2,576 pixels on the long edge, approximately 3.75 megapixels, versus 1.15 megapixels in Opus 4.6. In practical terms: before, you'd send a screenshot of your landing page and Claude would confuse small buttons with decorative text. Now it reads footer text at 8pt without making things up.

**Claude Design exports to what you already use.** You can export presentations and prototypes as PDF, URL, PPTX, or send them to Canva, where they remain fully editable and collaborative. It doesn't replace Canva or Figma; it's built for those who don't start with a design tool and need to convert an idea into something visual quickly.

**Reads your codebase for visual consistency.** During onboarding, Claude builds a design system for your team by reading your codebase and design files. Every project after that uses your colors, typography, and components automatically. We uploaded a client's assets (hardware store with WordPress site + logo in .ai + two old flyers). The second project we generated already used Pantone 485 C and Roboto Medium without us specifying it.

**Identical price, different tokenizer.** Opus 4.7 costs 5 USD per million input tokens and 25 USD output, exactly the same as Opus 4.6. But it uses a new tokenizer that can consume between 1× and 1.35× more tokens when processing text compared to previous models (up to ~35% more, varying by content). In our tests with 800-word Spanish briefs, consumption rose 18% vs. 4.6.

## The practical difference: before and after at Impulsa Lab

We tested with a real client: dental office in Queens, owner with no technical knowledge, zero budget for a designer.

**Before (manual Canva workflow, April 2026):**
- Owner writes bullet points in Google Doc: 45 min
- Searches for free Canva template that "more or less" matches logo colors: 30 min
- Copy-pastes text, adjusts font sizes slide by slide: 2.5 hours
- Exports PDF, reviews it, finds 4 spacing errors, edits again: 50 min
- **Total: 4 hours 45 min. Result: functional but generic.**

**After (Opus 4.7 + Claude Design, April 17, 2026):**
- Owner uploads logo .png + writes in chat: "Presentation for corporates, 10 slides, preventive and cosmetic dentistry services, warm professional tone, include before/after": 8 min
- Claude Design generates first complete version: 2 min
- Adjustments through conversation ("blue too dark, lighten it 20%," "add pricing slide"): 6 min
- Exports to PPTX, opens in PowerPoint, manual adjustment of one photo: 3 min
- **Total: 19 min. Result: consistent with brand, three layouts she never would have found in Canva.**

Measurable difference: 4h 26min saved. At 40 USD/hour (average SMB owner rate US according to BLS 2026), that's 177 USD in opportunity cost recovered per presentation.

## When NOT to use Claude Design

Anthropic is explicit: Opus 4.7 is less capable overall than their most powerful model, Claude Mythos Preview, and during its training they experimented with differentially reducing the cyber capabilities of 4.7. Put plainly: if your need is one of these three, stick with your current workflow:

**1. Brand identity design from scratch.** Claude Design assumes you already have logo, palette, typography. If you're at the "I need naming + isotype + brand manual" stage, hire a designer. What costs 1,500-3,500 USD once doesn't get replaced with a prompt.

**2. Documents of 100+ pages with complex layout.** Opus 4.7 improved at editing .docx with track changes and .pptx, and analyzing charts with image processing libraries, but a 200-page operations manual with indexes, cross-references, and nested tables is still work for InDesign + human.

**3. Direct export to social media or collaboration permissions.** You can export to PDF, PPTX, or Canva, but Claude doesn't schedule posts or invite users. If your agency manages 40 Instagram accounts with automated calendars, you need Later or Hootsuite, not this.

## The most common mistake we see

Teams adopting Claude Design assume "talking to AI" is enough. In 12 tests with clients, the ones that failed (3 of 12) shared the same pattern: generic prompt ("make a pretty presentation for my restaurant") without context.

A senior designer at Brilliant reported that the most complex pages required 20 or more prompts to recreate in competing tools, but only 2 in Claude Design. The difference: prompts with 3 specific components.

**Three fit conditions for direct output:**

1. **Explicit audience + tone.** Not "presentation for my restaurant." Yes: "presentation for corporate building owners in Manhattan, tone: direct professional, goal: monthly catering contracts for office lunches."

2. **Concrete input materials.** You can start from text prompt, upload images and documents (DOCX, PPTX, XLSX), point to your codebase, or use the web capture tool to grab elements directly from your website. Uploading logo .png + screenshot of current site reduces iterations from 8 to 2.

3. **Measurable constraints.** "10 slides maximum," "palette: only blue #003366 and gray #F5F5F5," "include pricing slide with 3×4 table." Datadog's product team described compressing a cycle that used to take a week of briefs, mockups, and review rounds into a single conversation, because each prompt brought quantifiable constraints.

## Three concrete tools that work today

**1. Sales presentations from brief (8-15 minutes end-to-end)**

Before you delivered static PDF with typos on slide 7. Now:
- Write brief in chat: audience, goal, 3-5 key points, tone
- Upload logo + (optional) screenshot of your site
- Claude Design generates complete presentation
- Export as PPTX or PDF, or send to Canva where it remains editable and collaborative
- Open in PowerPoint/Keynote, final adjustments (change photo, update figure)

Real case: Latin restaurant in Bronx needed presentation for school catering bid. 6-line brief + logo + menu in PDF. Output: 12 slides with consistent palette, health certifications section, testimonials slide with three-column layout. Time: 11 minutes. They won the bid (48K USD/year contract).

**2. Interactive app/web prototypes without Figma (25-45 minutes)**

What distinguishes Claude Design from previous experiments is the handoff mechanism. When a design is ready to build, Claude packages everything into a handoff bundle that can be passed to Claude Code with a single instruction, creating a closed loop from exploration to prototype to production code.

Real workflow (Queens dental office):
- Prompt: "mobile app for appointment scheduling, screens: login, available calendar, confirmation, reminders. Palette: soft mint green + white, minimalist icons"
- Upload logo + screenshot of current site (for consistency)
- Claude Design generates 3-4 interactive screens in preview
- Adjust through conversation ("'Schedule' button too small, 20% bigger")
- Export to standalone HTML or pass to Claude Code for implementation with single command

Deliverable: HTML file with functional click interactions, navigation between screens. Enough to show an investor or test with 10 patients. Time: 38 minutes. Previous alternative: hire Upwork freelancer (300-600 USD, 3-5 days).

**3. Commercial proposal one-pagers with live data (12-20 minutes)**

Opus 4.7 improved at producing and self-verifying tracked changes and slide layouts. We apply it like this:

- Prompt: "A4 one-pager for commercial cleaning service proposal, sections: who we are (3 lines), services (list 6 items), pricing (2-column table), contact. Layout: one column, wide margins, emphasis on pricing table"
- Upload logo .png
- Claude generates layout
- Export PDF, print or send email

Output includes self-verification: in 4 of 5 tests, Claude detected that contact info was missing before export and asked "do I add phone and email in footer?" In manual Canva workflow, you'd discover that error after printing 50 copies.

We measured the savings: a bodega in Harlem now generates 3 one-pagers per week (weekend promotions). Before: 0 (too much time). Now: 18 min/each, prints them and posts in windows. Increase in foot traffic: +12% in 3 weeks (data from infrared door counter, n=21 days).
